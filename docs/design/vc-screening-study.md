## The decision

> Which early-stage New Zealand startups should a hypothetical generalist seed/Series A fund
> prioritise for its next due-diligence slots?

It is a **screening and prioritisation** decision: which companies to spend scarce diligence time
on next. It is not an investment recommendation, a valuation, or a prediction of any company's
success. The fund, its mandate and its partners are hypothetical. The companies are real and every
fact about them comes from public sources, read on a fixed as-of date; every rating is the
author's own judgement against a written rubric.

**As-of date: 4 November 2026.** Every claim records its retrieval date. A source published after
the as-of date is not used; one older than 12 months is marked outdated by the existing freshness
rule and stays visible as such.

## Value tree and rubrics

Five objectives: one screened branch and four scored ones, with four leaves each (the historical
study had two). Every leaf uses **fixed anchors**: qualitative mappings are fixed by definition and
every numeric leaf states its domain, so adding or removing a company never moves another
company's value or class. Labels on every ordinal scale run from worst to best for the fund; a
"minimise" criterion is expressed by that order, because a qualitative mapping must not decrease
along the label order (specification §2).

Five-level rubrics map to 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1; four-step ladders to 0, 1/3, 2/3, 1. Equal
spacing is itself an owner judgement, stated in the README.

### Mandate (screened, not scored)

Rated **0 in both profiles**. Its leaves exist so the requirements check can read them.

| Leaf | Scale | Labels (worst → best) | Requirement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HQ country | categorical | New Zealand, Australia, Other | allowed {New Zealand} |
| Stage | ordinal | Pre-seed, Seed, Series A, Series B or later | allowed {Pre-seed, Seed, Series A} ("at most Series A") |
| Sector | categorical | Climate and energy; Health and medtech; Agritech and food; Fintech and B2B software; Space and advanced hardware; Consumer and marketplaces; Other | every label except Other |

**Stage rubric:** the last disclosed round, by the label in the primary source. "Pre-Series A" maps
to Seed. When no label is published, the stage is a *judgement* claim from the round's size and
reported dilution, and the evidence kind says so (Kea Aerospace).

**Does a zero-rated sibling group behave as intended?** Checked against the specification and the
code on 4 November 2026:

- Specification §3: "A zero rating is allowed for a sibling so long as the group sum is
  positive." The top-level group is Mandate 0 with four positive siblings, so it is valid, and
  every Mandate leaf's global weight is exactly 0. Inside the Mandate branch the three leaves need
  their own non-zero ratings (any equal values); their local weights are defined but their global
  weights stay 0.
- Mandate leaves still need a scale, a value function and a measurement for every company:
  validation reports `MISSING_MEASUREMENT` for any leaf. Their value functions are inert. One
  side effect: the Stage scale is ordinal, so its mapping must increase along the label order
  (Series B or later = 1). With weight 0 this never affects a value.
- **Three side effects to accept:**
  1. *One-way sensitivity:* the Mandate node is eligible (local weight 0 < 1), and raising it from
     0 changes values. The Sensitivity tab can list thresholds for "giving the mandate a score",
     which would favour Hnry through the inert Stage mapping. The
     [screening summary](screening-summary.md) excludes zero-weight nodes from fragility (its
     decision 2). Owner-approved showcase decision D3 (5 November 2026) applies that same
     exclusion to Open Questions; the interactive sensitivity engine remains unchanged.
  2. *Dominance:* the diagnostic compares partial values on every leaf, including inert ones, so a
     dominance relation can be broken by a Mandate leaf. It is reported, never applied; the README
     notes it.
  3. *PROMETHEE II:* each Mandate leaf needs an entry; with weight 0 it raises the existing
     `PROMETHEE_CRITERION_INERT` warning three times. That is the intended reading.

This behaves as intended, so no alternative structure is proposed.

### Team

| Leaf | Rubric (level 1 → 5) |
| --- | --- |
| Founder–problem expertise | 1 no public evidence of domain experience; 2 adjacent field or under 3 years in the domain; 3 at least 3 years in the target industry or its research field; 4 recognised domain expert (doctorate plus 5 years, or senior operating role in the target industry); 5 the founder's own prior work is the basis of the core product, plus commercial experience in the target industry |
| Team completeness | Roles: technical, commercial and domain/regulatory lead, each a named person. 1 one role named; 2 two; 3 all three, one part-time or advisory; 4 all three full-time; 5 all three plus named leads for the next stage's main function (for example manufacturing, regulatory, sales) |
| Prior venture or operating track record | 1 none public; 2 prior venture with no known outcome, or 5+ years operating experience; 3 senior role in a company of 100+ staff, or a prior venture that raised outside capital; 4 prior venture with a known exit, or executive of a scaled company; 5 two or more of level 4 across the founding team |
| Advisors, board and key hires | 1 none named; 2 named, without relevant domain or investor experience; 3 an independent chair or one relevant domain advisor; 4 a board with relevant sector and investor directors; 5 level 4 plus a named senior hire from a leading firm in the target industry |

### Problem and solution

| Leaf | Rubric |
| --- | --- |
| Problem severity and willingness to pay | 1 problem stated only by the company; 2 independent source confirms the problem; 3 independent source quantifies its cost to the target customer; 4 level 3 plus a named budget holder or obligation (regulation, reimbursement, procurement line); 5 level 4 plus evidence that customers already pay for an inferior substitute |
| Differentiation and defensibility | 1 none stated; 2 differentiation claimed, no protectable asset; 3 filed patent, exclusive licence or proprietary dataset; 4 granted patent or exclusive licence in a major market; 5 level 4 plus a compounding advantage (network, data or regulatory moat) evidenced publicly |
| Product or technology readiness | Ladder: Concept → Prototype → Pilot-ready → Commercial (sold and delivered in normal operation) |
| Regulatory and certification burden (minimise) | Ladder, worst → best: Approval required, pathway not started → Approval required, pathway under way → Approval obtained in one major market or certification only → No approval or certification required |

Readiness reads only the technical state, and the regulatory leaf only the approval path, so the
two do not count the same thing twice; "technical risk" lives in readiness.

### Market

| Leaf | Scale and rubric |
| --- | --- |
| Addressable market size | Ratio, US$ billions, the company's **initial serviceable market** as defined in the README, from cited third-party estimates. Fixed domain [0.01, 200]. Concave piecewise-linear value: (0.01, 0), (0.1, 0.15), (1, 0.35), (5, 0.65), (25, 0.9), (200, 1) — *owner judgement*: size matters most up to venture scale. A company-stated figure is a *proxy* claim and disputed |
| Market growth | Ratio, % CAGR of that same market from the same report, fixed domain [0, 40], linear increasing |
| Competitive intensity (minimise) | Ladder, worst → best: Several funded leaders (3+ direct competitors with US$10m+ disclosed funding) → One or two funded leaders → Direct competitors, none funded at that level → No direct competitor identified |
| Route to global markets | 1 no offshore route stated; 2 target offshore markets named; 3 offshore approval, accreditation or channel partner process under way; 4 approval or accreditation obtained, or an offshore entity operating; 5 operating in two or more offshore markets with local staff |

Route to market is about *access* (approvals, accreditation, channels, entities), not customers,
which belong to Traction.

### Traction

| Leaf | Scale and rubric |
| --- | --- |
| Customer validation | Ladder: Company claim only → Unpaid trial → Paid pilot → Production customers. Reads the strongest *independently verifiable* customer evidence |
| Commercial stage | Ladder: Pre-revenue → First revenue → Repeatable revenue (multiple customers, recurring) → Scaling (stated growth with revenue in several markets) |
| Strategic partnerships and channels | 1 none public; 2 announced MoU or research collaboration; 3 named non-customer partner in an active programme; 4 commercial, distribution or manufacturing agreement with an established firm; 5 two or more at level 4 |
| Milestone delivery | 1 no dated public milestone; 2 dated milestones announced, none yet due; 3 at least one dated milestone delivered, others late or missed; 4 dated milestones delivered on or near plan; 5 two or more delivered on plan |

Customer validation and commercial stage are correlated: validation is the *strength* of the best
customer evidence, commercial stage its *breadth and repeatability*. The profiles weight them
differently on purpose; the README states the correlation.

### What is deliberately not a criterion

- **Funding raised or valuation:** an outcome of other investors' judgements and largely a
  function of stage and traction; including it would double count.
- **Media attention, awards and accelerator badges:** not evidence of any rubric level on their own.
- **Founder demographics or personal attributes:** excluded.

### Cells without public evidence

Every scored leaf must have a measurement for any method to run (`MISSING_MEASUREMENT`), so a
missing cell would make the whole flagship unevaluable. **Decision 1** below chooses how the study
handles a rubric cell for which no public evidence exists. The recommendation is that every rubric
defines its lowest level as an observable statement about the public record ("no public evidence
of …", "none named"), recorded as a *judgement* claim citing the pages searched, so that Open
Questions lists it as unsupported. A numeric cell (market size, growth) without a cited estimate
is **not** filled: the study stops and asks the owner, as the run's rules require.

## Methods on this case

### Value-based sorting

Classes, best first: **Prioritise for diligence** (value ≥ 0.60), **Monitor** (≥ 0.40), **Not
now** (< 0.40). Deliberately not Invest/Pass: these are real companies and this is a queue.

Why these boundaries (*owner judgement*): with fixed anchors, a company at the middle level of
every rubric scores 0.5 in either profile. Prioritise therefore means "clearly above the middle of
the scale on the weighted whole" (roughly level 4 of 5 on the heaviest criteria); Not now means
"below the middle on most of what this partner weights". The boundaries are fixed **before** any
company is rated and are not tuned afterwards; if the owner wants different cut-offs they are
changed here, before Phase 2 scoring. The existing local-anchor warning does not fire because no
leaf uses local anchors.

### SMAA-2

Uncertainty only where evidence genuinely disagrees or a rating is judgement-heavy, never for
missing data:

- **Market size and growth:** where two or more cited estimates of the same market differ, a
  `uniform` distribution on [lowest, highest] estimate for that cell; where one source exists, the
  cell stays fixed. Estimates from the same report family share a draw (`shared-draw group`), the
  only dependence stated.
- **Judgement-heavy rubric cells:** for cells whose claim kind is *judgement* (not *observed*), a
  `categorical` distribution of ±1 rubric level: 0.25 / 0.5 / 0.25 (0.5 / 0.5 at a scale end). The
  README lists every such cell.
- **Weights:** a separate preference-uncertainty run with `rating_intervals` spanning the two
  partners' ratings at the top level (Team 80–90, P&S 60–100, Market 60–80, Traction 50–100),
  everything below fixed to the Technical/IP profile. It answers "if the partnership has not
  settled its weights, how often does each company lead?"

Every result keeps its existing label: sampled acceptability under these assumptions.

### PROMETHEE II

Its own inputs, never derived from the swing profiles:

- **Weights:** each objective 25, split equally across its four leaves (6.25 each); Mandate leaves
  0 (three expected `PROMETHEE_CRITERION_INERT` warnings). A deliberately plain "equal objectives"
  reading, so the cross-check differs from both partners in a stated way.
- **Codings:** rubric levels 1–5 and ladder steps 1–4, increasing with label order.
- **Preference functions:** rubric and ladder leaves V-shape with p = 2 levels (one level apart is
  half a preference); market size linear with q = 1, p = 20 (US$ bn); growth linear with q = 2,
  p = 10 (percentage points).

### AHP

Not set up: 8 companies need 28 pairwise company judgements for each of the 16 scored criteria
(448 in all) that nobody made. Compare says so in plain language (cleanup item 3); no `ahp` section is written.

### Requirements

The three mandate requirements above. Hnry fails "Stage at most Series A"; everyone else passes
on the proposed facts. Hnry keeps its place in every ranking with the failure beside it.

## Decisions

1. **Cells without public evidence.** *Recommended:* each rubric's lowest level is an observable
   statement about the public record, entered as a *judgement* claim citing the pages searched and
   listed by Open Questions; numeric cells without a cited estimate stop the run for an owner
   decision. *Alternative:* leave such cells missing, which is truer to "missing is not zero" but
   makes MAVT, sorting, SMAA-2 and PROMETHEE II unavailable for the whole study until filled.
2. **The eight companies.** *Recommended:* the eight above, subject to the owner's conflict
   review. *Alternatives:* any alternate in place of any of them; Halter in place of Hnry.
3. **Sector requirement.** *Recommended:* allow every listed sector, excluding only "Other", so
   only stage fails. *Alternative:* exclude one sector from the fund's list to show a second
   failure reason, at the cost of a contrived mandate.
4. **Swing ratings and class boundaries.** *Recommended:* as tabled, fixed before scoring.
5. **Scenario.** *Recommended:* include "Linear market size". *Alternative:* none.
6. **Gallery.** *Recommended:* a new starter directory for the study; the invented starter
   retitled. *Alternative:* replace the invented starter, losing its proposal and model workflows.

## Owner decisions during the build

Recorded 4 November 2026, after the decisions above were accepted as recommended.

1. **Market-size rule.** Public estimates for the same segment differ by up to three orders of
   magnitude between publishers. Each company's market is the narrowest named third-party segment
   containing its core product; the entered value is the median of distinct publishers' 2025 values
   stated on their public pages, and growth the median of the same publishers' forecast CAGRs.
